Customer Hub — Why We Need This

The case for a unified customer hub, and what it changes for the people who use our products every day.

Bottom line up front

Today, a Momentus customer's relationship with us is scattered across five disconnected logins and five disconnected experiences. Nothing today tells them what to do next, or why. The Customer Hub fixes both — one door in, and a system that actively guides them toward the value they're already paying for but not using.

The problem today

  • Five logins, five mental models. Learning, support, community, rewards, and events each live in a different vendor's product, with different navigation, different branding, and no shared identity. Customers re-learn "where things live" five times over.
  • No single place to see what's new. A product release, a support workflow change, an upcoming webinar — each is announced (if at all) inside its own silo. Customers miss things that directly affect how they use the product.
  • Adoption gaps are invisible until a renewal conversation. We can see a customer isn't using Functions, or hasn't touched a feature they're paying for — but today nothing in their day-to-day experience tells them that, or offers to help.
  • Engagement is passive. Customers come to us when something breaks (support) or when they're required to (a course). There's no reason to open a Momentus product just to see what's relevant to them right now.

What the Hub changes

One login, one home base

A single sign-in unlocks Learning, Support, Community, Rewards, and Events. The customer stops thinking in terms of five separate vendor products and starts thinking in terms of one relationship with Momentus.

An adoption agent, not a static portal

The Featured/Recommended page is the strategic core of this product. Every card is tied to a real signal — low usage of a feature, an unfinished mission, a relevant upcoming webinar — and explains itself: "Low adoption · Functions → Hey, missing out on this value? Try Functions." This turns the hub into an always-on nudge system that proactively closes adoption gaps, instead of waiting for a QBR to surface them.

A reason to come back

Announcements, What's New, and opt-in email digests (instant/weekly/monthly) give customers a lightweight reason to check in regularly — not just when something's broken. More visits means more chances for the adoption agent to do its job.

One search across everything

A single search bar spanning courses, KB articles, discussions, missions, rewards, and events means the customer never has to guess which of five products holds the answer they're looking for.

Why this matters for us

  • Adoption drives retention. Customers who use more of what they're paying for renew at higher rates and expand more readily — this gives us a scalable, always-on channel to close adoption gaps instead of relying solely on CSMs.
  • Lower support load. A single, well-surfaced knowledge base and community reduce ticket volume for questions customers can self-serve.
  • Stronger brand cohesion. Right now, a customer's day-to-day experience of "Momentus" is really five different vendors' UIs. The Hub makes the relationship feel like one product, one brand, one company that's paying attention to them.
  • A new targeting channel. Once this exists, it becomes the place we launch product announcements, betas, and campaigns directly to engaged customers — not just through email or a CSM's calendar.

Go-Live Roadmap

A phased plan to take the Customer Hub from prototype to production.

This is primarily an identity, integration, and content-partnership program — not a rebuild of any of the five destinations. Realistic total timeline is 6–9 months to full production, assuming vendor SSO/API access is confirmed early. The two items most likely to move that date are SSO contract-tier confirmation per vendor and the unified search build-vs-partner decision.

Phase 0 — Decisions before any build work (2–3 weeks)

  • Pick the identity provider (Okta / Entra ID / Auth0) if we don't already standardize on one.
  • Confirm SSO + SCIM availability per vendor plan — 360Learning, the Ungerboeck/Zendesk support centers, Zealot, and the rewards/missions platform.
  • Decide build-vs-buy for unified search (Phase 3) — the single biggest scope driver in the program.
  • Name an owner for role/permission mapping across systems.

Phase 1 — Identity foundation (4–6 weeks)

Stand up the hub as an SSO client against the chosen IdP; register each destination as a trusting service provider over SAML 2.0 or OIDC. Standardize on one identifier (corporate email) and enable SCIM sync where supported.

Phase 2 — Native content integration, page by page (8–12 weeks, parallelizable)

Phase 3 — Universal search (parallel track, 8–16+ weeks)

Federated search (query each system live, merge at request time) is the recommended starting path — fastest to stand up and inherits each system's native permissions. A central index or a buy/partner product (e.g., Glean, Coveo) are the alternatives if federated search proves too slow or too limited.

Phase 4 — Notifications & digest emails (4–6 weeks, parallel to Phase 2)

Stand up a notification-preferences data model and subscription store, then wire a scheduled email job to the existing marketing/transactional platform to render the three digest templates from real aggregated content.

Phase 5 — Pilot, then rollout (4–6 weeks)

Pilot with one customer segment; validate SSO across all five systems in production. Confirm deprovisioning actually revokes access everywhere before wider release, then roll out in stages by customer segment.

Possible Blockers and How We Navigate Them

The realistic risks in this program, and the mitigation for each.

Vendor SSO/SCIM is gated behind a higher contract tier

Navigate by: confirming plan requirements with each vendor's account team in Phase 0, before any engineering commitment — this is a procurement conversation, not a technical one, and it's the single most common surprise in programs like this.

True embedding of 360Learning, Zendesk/Ungerboeck, or Zealot is not possible

These platforms set frame-blocking headers (X-Frame-Options/CSP) we can't override. Navigate by: accepting this as permanent — the Hub links out to real destinations in-context with matching visual styling, rather than embedding them pixel-for-pixel. This is what the prototype already does.

Unified search is harder than it looks

Doing this well — across five access-controlled systems, respecting each user's permissions — is not something we'd recommend building from scratch on a first release. Navigate by: starting with federated search (Phase 3) and revisiting a central index or a buy/partner tool only once real usage data justifies the investment.

Role/permission mapping across systems is inconsistent

An "admin" in 360Learning isn't an "admin" in Zendesk. Navigate by: assigning a single accountable owner (Phase 0) to define and maintain the IdP-group-to-system-role mapping before Phase 1 begins, not after issues surface.

The adoption-agent recommendations need real usage data we may not currently pipe anywhere central

This is the biggest net-new engineering effort in the program, not a simple integration. Navigate by: scoping an initial, smaller set of high-confidence signals (e.g., feature-usage flags we already track) rather than attempting full behavioral analytics on day one — expand the signal set post-launch.

Deprovisioning gaps — access not actually revoked everywhere on offboarding

Navigate by: explicitly testing offboarding end-to-end during the Phase 5 pilot, in production — this is the scenario most likely to be missed if only tested in staging.
